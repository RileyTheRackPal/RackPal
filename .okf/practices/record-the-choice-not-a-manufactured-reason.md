# The shape

A user picks an option from a list. The ruling then records not only the choice but a REASON, and
the reason is the one the option's author had in mind when writing the list. Nobody lied; the
sentence simply grew a clause nobody said.

- **DR-183**: canon put the author's rationale in the user's voice. Caught in review.
- **DR-187 §11.186.2**: *"It lands as its own undo entry beside the edit, **which was stated in the
  question and chosen with it**."* The consequence of that shape was never shown to anybody. Built as
  written, the first `Ctrl+Z` after any edit took back the schematic's ARRANGEMENT and left the edit,
  so the sheet scrambled and undoing one delete cost two presses, which is exactly what DR-187 exists
  to abolish. Two tests older than the ruling caught it. §11.186.2.1 carries the correction.

# Why it matters more than it sounds

**A recorded reason is load-bearing.** Canon is normative, and a later reader, including a later
session of the same author, treats *"chosen with it"* as settled and builds to it without re-testing
it. A choice can be re-asked cheaply. A reason attributed to somebody else cannot: it has the
authority of a decision and none of the evidence.

# What to do

- Write what was chosen, in the fewest words that identify the option.
- Write the consequences **separately**, as measurements, in the author's own voice, and mark them as
  such: *"measured"*, *"probed"*, *"asserted by"*. That way a wrong one is a wrong measurement, which
  gets re-run, rather than a wrong decision, which gets obeyed.
- If a clause states a consequence nobody was shown, either show it and ask, or state it as the
  author's own claim and let it be refutable. **The refutable version is the one that gets refuted
  before it ships.**
- "Stated in the question" is checkable. Check it against what was actually asked before writing it.
